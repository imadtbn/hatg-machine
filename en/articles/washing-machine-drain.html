Washing Machine Won't Drain: Safe Step-by-Step Diagnosis

Washing Machine Faults August 8, 2026 7 min read
Safety alert

Disconnect power and shut off the water source before cleaning or inspecting. Do not open gas, refrigeration, or high-voltage circuits without a qualified technician.

Failure to drain can result from a simple blockage, a kinked hose, or a nonworking pump. Don’t repeatedly restart the cycle; drain the water safely first, and disconnect power before accessing the filter or underneath the appliance.

Initial check

Check that the drain or spin program hasn't been canceled, then examine the drain hose at the back of the machine. It should not be kinked or pinched, and it should not be pushed too far into the standpipe. After disconnecting power, slowly open the pump filter with a towel and a low container underneath, because water may come out in a rush.

Remove lint, coins, and buttons, and gently move the pump impeller from the outside if the manual allows. Do not disassemble the pump or short the wiring. After securely reinstalling the filter, run a short drain test without laundry.

When to stop?

If you hear a humming with no rotation, water returns, a leak appears, or the error code repeats after cleaning the filter, the fault may be in the pump, the water level sensor, or the control board. Stop the appliance and call a technician, especially if access requires tilting the washer or removing the top cover.

The principles of home electrical safety warn against working on an appliance that is connected to electricity or near water; make safety a precondition before any diagnosis.
