When to Fix the Appliance Yourself and When to Call a Technician?

Safety August 11, 2026 7 min read
Safety alert

Disconnect power and shut off the water source before cleaning or inspecting. Do not open gas, refrigeration, or high-voltage circuits without a qualified technician.

Good home diagnosis knows its limits. You can often clean a filter, rearrange a load, or inspect a visible hose, but you don't need to prove your skill by disassembling an electrical control panel or a refrigeration circuit. The cost of a qualified technician is less than the risk of shock, fire, or damaging the entire appliance.

Low-risk household tasks

Unplugging, reading the manual, cleaning permitted filters, checking for kinked hoses, adjusting the feet, and recording an error code are reasonable first steps if the appliance is unplugged and there is no hazardous leak. Use non-conductive tools, and do not bypass safety devices.

Call a technician immediately when

  • a burning smell, smoke, sparking, or excessive heat at the plug.
  • repeated tripping of a breaker or electric shock when touching the casing.
  • water leaking near an outlet or electrical panel.
  • the need to open a refrigeration circuit or handle refrigerant or pressurized gas.
  • the error code recurs after following the manual steps or the fault immediately returns.

ESFI recommends unplugging damaged small appliances and repairing or replacing them, and the CPSC website allows checking recalls and safety warnings. Check your country and brand because warranty and recall procedures vary.

How to choose a technician?

Ask for a license or certification number where applicable, a written description of the diagnosis and cost, and a warranty on the work. Prepare the model number, a photo of the error code, and a record of what was tried, and do not allow bypassing a sensor or fuse as a permanent fix.
