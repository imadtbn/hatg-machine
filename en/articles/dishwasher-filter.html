Cleaning the Dishwasher Filter: The Right Method and When to Replace It

Dishwashers August 7, 2026 6 min read
Safety alert

Disconnect power and shut off the water source before cleaning or inspecting. Do not open gas, refrigeration, or high-voltage circuits without a qualified technician.

The filter is the first line of defense against food debris. When it clogs, water flow decreases and residues return to the dishes; noise may increase or water may remain at the bottom. The removal method varies, so use the model manual and do not force the ring if it does not move.

Cleaning method

  1. Stop the cycle and unplug or switch off the circuit breaker, then remove the lower rack.
  2. Remove the filter according to the locking direction, and empty debris into the trash not the sink.
  3. Rinse it with lukewarm water and a soft brush, without metal wire that could damage the mesh.
  4. Inspect the pump area very carefully for glass or bone, and do not insert your fingers deeply.
  5. Reinstall the filter and secure it tightly; running without it may allow debris to reach the pump.

Clean the spray arms if the openings are blocked, then run an empty cycle with a dedicated cleaner if needed. Do not mix cleaning agents or use a cleaner that causes excessive foaming. If odor or standing water remains after cleaning the filter, check the drain hose or call a technician.

When to replace the filter?

Replace it if the mesh is broken, it no longer locks in place, or cracks appear that allow debris to pass. Buying an original part or one compatible with the model number is better than a generic part that doesn't match the lock or openings.
