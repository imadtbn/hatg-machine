Fridge not cooling well? A practical check before calling a technician

Refrigerators August 14, 2026 8 min read
Safety alert

Disconnect power and shut off the water source before cleaning or inspecting. Do not open gas, refrigeration, or high-voltage circuits without a qualified technician.

When a refrigerator's cooling weakens, don't start by randomly changing the thermostat or adding refrigerant. Begin by measuring the temperature and checking airflow and the door, because the cause may be an incorrect setting or stacking that prevents air circulation. The FDA recommends keeping the refrigerator compartment at 4 °C or below, and the freezer around −18 °C, using an independent thermometer if the digital readout is absent.

Start by measuring temperature, not guessing

Place a thermometer made for appliances in the middle of the refrigerator compartment, away from the door, and leave it for the time recommended by the manufacturer. Do not rely on the temperature of a single interior wall; measuring air and food in more than one location reveals whether the problem is overall or confined to a specific spot. If the temperature is high, move perishable foods to a cold place and follow food safety rules before continuing diagnosis.

Simple causes you can check safely

  • Ensure air distribution vents are not blocked by cans or bags, and do not overfill shelves so as to prevent air circulation.
  • Inspect the door gasket for cracks or gaps. Close the door on a sheet of paper; if it slides out easily from multiple spots the seal may not be adequate.
  • Keep the refrigerator away from the oven and direct sunlight and leave clearance behind it according to the model manual. ENERGY STAR points to the importance of clearance, ventilation and checking the gasket.
  • If the model allows condenser coil cleaning, unplug and remove dust with a soft brush or vacuum, and do not remove covers or touch exposed wiring.

When does the fault require a technician?

Call a qualified refrigeration technician if the temperature remains high after adjusting ventilation and seals, or if there is an unusual sound from the compressor, or heavy ice buildup on one area, or signs of oil and leakage. Do not pierce ice with a sharp tool or open the refrigeration circuit; these actions can cause damage, leaks and safety hazards.

Tip to reduce energy use

Reduce how long the door is open, let hot food cool safely before putting it inside, and clean spills promptly. Also see the article Energy saving in home appliances and the article Routine maintenance checklist to develop a regular follow-up schedule.
