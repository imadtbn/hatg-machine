Dryer not drying clothes? Causes of poor drying and safe steps

Dryers August 13, 2026 8 min read
Safety alert

Disconnect power and shut off the water source before cleaning or inspecting. Do not open gas, refrigeration, or high-voltage circuits without a qualified technician.

If a dryer cycle finishes and clothes remain damp, raising the heat is often not the solution. Drying depends on sufficient air passing through the clothes and the exhaust of moist air; therefore a clogged lint filter or duct leads to longer cycles and higher temperatures. The U.S. Fire Administration notes that lack of cleaning was a major factor in a significant share of clothes dryer fires, so poor drying should be treated as both a performance and a safety issue.

Initial check before starting a new cycle

Unplug the dryer, then clean the lint filter as described in the model's manual. Visually inspect it for any layer blocking airflow, and clean the surrounding area without pushing lint into the machine. Do not use the dryer if you smell burning, see smoke, or notice abnormal heat at the plug.

Check the airflow path

  • Make sure the rear hose is not kinked or crushed behind the appliance.
  • Check that the external exhaust vent opens during operation and that there is not an extremely weak airflow coming out.
  • Do not block the vent and do not replace the duct with non-approved materials or one that is excessively long.
  • If the run is long or reaches a high location, make deep cleaning a job for a technician or a specialized service.

Load and cycle

Separate heavy loads like towels, and do not mix them with very lightweight items. Overloading prevents clothes from tumbling and reduces airflow, while very small loads may cause the sensor to end the cycle early on some models. Choose a program that matches the fabric type and desired dryness level, and follow the garment care labels.

Signs that require stopping

Stop immediately if you smell burning, the dryer cabinet becomes excessively hot, you hear rubbing or grinding noises, or the circuit breaker trips repeatedly. Do not use an extension cord with a dryer; the Fire Administration recommends plugging large appliances directly into a wall outlet and not using temporary cords. After any repair, run a short test cycle and monitor the airflow instead of leaving the appliance operating unattended.

Relation of the problem to energy use

A clogged run makes cycles longer and increases energy consumption. After cleaning it, monitor drying time for a similar load, and note any change in sound or temperature. Also see the article Energy saving for general practices that reduce appliance runtime without compromising safety.
