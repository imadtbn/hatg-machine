Dishwasher leaving a white film? Causes of deposits and solutions

Dishwashers August 11, 2026 7 min read
Safety alert

Disconnect power and shut off the water source before cleaning or inspecting. Do not open gas, refrigeration, or high-voltage circuits without a qualified technician.

The white film on cups or the dishwasher interior may be salt deposits from hard water, undissolved detergent residue, or the result of a program with unsuitable temperature and water flow. Do not start by replacing the pump; first check the type and location of the deposit and the salt and rinse aid settings according to the model's manual.

How to distinguish the type of deposit?

If the residue is powdery and appears on surfaces after a short cycle, it may be related to the detergent dose or poor dissolution. If it is cloudy or chalky and recurring on glass, it may be related to hard water or low salt in models that use a softening system. Try cleaning a single item using the method recommended in the manual, and do not mix different chemicals.

Practical steps to improve

  • Clean the filter and spray arms, and make sure their holes are not blocked by food debris.
  • Check for dishwasher-specific salt if the model has a salt tank, and do not use table salt instead.
  • Adjust the rinse aid according to your water hardness and the manufacturer's instructions, and avoid increasing the dose arbitrarily.
  • Load dishes so they do not block the spray arms, and choose a program suited to the level of soiling.
  • Do not fully rinse dishes before loading; ENERGY STAR recommends scraping off leftovers and running a full load instead of pre-rinsing.

When do you need a technician?

If the residue remains after cleaning the filter and spray arms and adjusting consumables, or if there is a heating or leaking problem or a clear loss of spray pressure, call a technician. Do not open the heater or tamper with the drain pump; disconnect the appliance and water before any external inspection.

Reduce consumption with better results

ENERGY STAR notes that the no-heat drying option combined with rinse aid can give good results with lower energy use in some models. Test it on a full load, and consult the dishwasher filter cleaning guide if you notice weak spray or odors.
