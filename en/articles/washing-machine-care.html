Washing Machine Care: A Practical Guide to Extending Its Lifespan

Washing machines August 3, 2026 8 min read
Safety alert

Disconnect power and shut off the water source before cleaning or inspecting. Do not open gas, refrigeration, or high-voltage circuits without a qualified technician.

A washing machine runs efficiently when water can enter and drain freely, loads stay balanced, and moisture-retaining parts dry out. Most odor or vibration problems start from simple operating habits rather than a direct electronic fault.

After every cycle

Remove clothes promptly, wipe water from the door seal, and leave the door and detergent drawer partly open. Do not use more detergent than recommended; excess suds can leave residues and affect rinsing. Check pockets before washing because coins and pins can clog the pump or scratch the drum.

Monthly cleaning

  1. Run the tub-cleaning cycle if available, or follow the manufacturer's instructions for a suitable empty-cycle clean.
  2. Clean the detergent drawer with a soft brush and dry it before replacing it.
  3. Check the pump filter after disconnecting power and placing a towel and a shallow container if the design allows it.
  4. Inspect the inlet and drain hoses for bulges, cracks, or kinks.

Do not mix cleaning chemicals, and do not use vinegar and bleach together. Read the product label and model instructions; some seals and finishes are affected by strong chemicals. If a leak appears from the pump housing or an error code persists after cleaning, stop the machine and contact a technician.

Load and balance

Distribute large items with medium ones and do not pack the drum to its limit. An unbalanced load increases vibration and can strain shock absorbers and bearings. The washer should be level on a solid floor, with all four feet firmly contacting the ground.
